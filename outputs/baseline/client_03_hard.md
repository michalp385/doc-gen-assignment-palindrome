We recommend disinvesting the funds in the joint GIA. The current value of this account is £30,000, and we will be using that along with the £120,000 inheritance to fund the top-ups and establish a new joint investment account. 

Considering the sale of the GIA, the estimated capital gains tax (CGT) liability needs to be assessed. Assuming the GIA has a negligible cost basis (i.e., it was acquired at a low value relative to its current worth), the potential gain from the sale of the GIA may be close to the entire amount of £30,000. However, each individual is entitled to an annual exempt amount; for the 2026-2027 tax year, this is typically around £12,300.

If we assume that neither Robert nor Jean has utilised their annual exemptions for the year, the applicable taxable gain would be £30,000 - £12,300 = £17,700. The capital gains tax rate for higher rate taxpayers is generally 20%. Therefore, an approximate estimate of the CGT liability arising from the sale of the GIA would be £17,700 x 20% = £3,540.

This comprehensive understanding of your current financial position and the potential tax implications is crucial as we move forward with your investment strategy.
